# Refined Donaldson–Thomas invariants of symmetric quivers — literature dossier

**Scope.** State of the art on quantized/refined/motivic DT invariants of symmetric quivers, with special focus on the $m$-loop quiver $Q_m$ (one vertex, $m$ loops). Compiled 2026-07-31 from primary sources (arXiv full texts were extracted and read; every formula below is transcribed from the named source in that source's own normalization, except where explicitly marked **[verified computation]** — those were recomputed symbolically from the quoted definitions as an independent cross-check).

**Convention health warning (read first).** The literature uses at least four inequivalent normalizations of "the" quiver partition function and of $\Omega_d(q)$, differing by $q \leftrightarrow q^{-1}$, $q \to q^2$, sign twists $(-1)^{\chi}$, and $t$-rescalings $t \to q^{\pm(m-1)/2}t$. Each section below records the exact convention of its source. Section 9 gives a conversion dictionary and parallel numerical tables. One known misprint is documented in §4.6.

## 1. Kontsevich–Soibelman: CoHA and refined DT invariants (arXiv:1006.2706)

M. Kontsevich, Y. Soibelman, *Cohomological Hall algebra, exponential Hodge structures and motivic Donaldson–Thomas invariants*, arXiv:1006.2706, Commun. Number Theory Phys. 5 (2011) 231–352.

### 1.1 CoHA of the $d$-loop quiver (KS §2.5)

For $Q = Q_d$ (one vertex, $d\ge0$ loops), $\mathcal H = \bigoplus_n H^\bullet_{GL(n,\mathbb C)}(\mathbb A^{dn^2})$ carries the shuffle product (on symmetric polynomials, $f_1$ in $n$, $f_2$ in $m$ variables):

$$(f_1\cdot f_2)(x_1,\dots,x_{n+m}) := \sum_{\substack{i_1<\dots<i_n,\; j_1<\dots<j_m\\ \{i\}\sqcup\{j\}=\{1,\dots,n+m\}}} f_1(x_{i_1},\dots,x_{i_n})\, f_2(x_{j_1},\dots,x_{j_m}) \left(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{l=1}^{m}(x_{j_l}-x_{i_k})\right)^{d-1}.$$

Bigrading: a homogeneous symmetric polynomial of degree $k$ in $n$ variables has bidegree $(n,\, 2k+(1-d)n^2)$. The algebra is commutative for $d$ odd, supercommutative for $d$ even.

**Partition function (KS §2.5, exact convention).** The Hilbert–Poincaré series of the bigraded algebra twisted by $(-1)^{\text{parity}}$:

$$P_d = P_d(z,q^{1/2}) = \sum_{n\ge0,\,m\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^m \dim(\mathcal H_{n,m})\, z^n q^{m/2} \;=\; \sum_{n\ge 0}\frac{(-q^{1/2})^{(1-d)n^2}}{(1-q)\cdots(1-q^n)}\, z^n \;\in\; \mathbb Z((q^{1/2}))[[z]].$$

Note $(1-d)n^2 = \chi_{Q_d}(n,n)$, so the numerator is $(-q^{1/2})^{\chi(n,n)}$ and the Pochhammer is in $q$ (not $q^{-1}$). Special cases: $P_0 = (q^{1/2}z;q)_\infty$, $P_1 = 1/(z;q)_\infty$, with $(x;q)_\infty := \prod_{i\ge0}(1-q^i x)$.

**Theorem 3 (KS §2.5) — refined DT invariants of the $d$-loop quiver.** For any $d\ge0$ there exist integers $\delta^{(d)}(n,m)$, $n\ge1$, $m\in (d-1)n+2\mathbb Z$, nonzero for finitely many $m$ at fixed $n$, with

$$P_d = \prod_{n\ge1}\prod_{m\in\mathbb Z}\left(q^{m/2}z^n;q\right)_\infty^{\delta^{(d)}(n,m)},$$

hence

$$\frac{P_d(z,q^{1/2})}{P_d(qz,q^{1/2})} = \prod_{n\ge1}\prod_{m\in\mathbb Z}\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(1-q^{m/2+i}z^n\right)^{\delta^{(d)}(n,m)},$$

and in the classical limit $P^{cl}_d(z) := \lim_{q^{1/2}\to1} P_d(z)/P_d(qz) = \prod_{n\ge1}(1-z^n)^{n\,c^{(d)}(n)}$ with $c^{(d)}(n) = \sum_m \delta^{(d)}(n,m) \in \mathbb Z$. KS note (citing Reineke) that $P^{cl}_d$ is algebraic:

$$P^{cl}_d(z) = 1+(-1)^{d-1} z\,\bigl(P^{cl}_d(z)\bigr)^d.$$

### 1.2 Symmetric quivers, the sign twist, and Conjecture 1 (KS §2.6)

For symmetric $Q$ (adjacency matrix $a_{ij}=a_{ji}$), grading $\mathcal H_{\gamma,k} := H^{k-\chi_Q(\gamma,\gamma)}(BG_\gamma)$, where $\chi_Q(\gamma_1,\gamma_2) = \sum_i \gamma_1^i\gamma_2^i - \sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\gamma_1^i\gamma_2^j$. One has $a_{\gamma,k}\cdot a_{\gamma',k'} = (-1)^{\chi_Q(\gamma,\gamma')} a_{\gamma',k'}\cdot a_{\gamma,k}$; a bilinear cocycle $\psi$ on $(\mathbb Z/2)^I$ with $\psi(\gamma_1,\gamma_2)+\psi(\gamma_2,\gamma_1)=\chi_Q(\gamma_1,\gamma_2)+\epsilon(\gamma_1)\epsilon(\gamma_2) \bmod 2$ defines the modified product $a_1\star a_2 := (-1)^{\psi(\gamma_1,\gamma_2)}a_1\cdot a_2$, making $(\mathcal H,\star)$ a $\mathbb Z^I_{\ge0}\times\mathbb Z$-graded **supercommutative** algebra (parity $= \chi_Q(\gamma,\gamma) \bmod 2 = k \bmod 2$).

**Conjecture 1 (KS §2.6; proved by Efimov).** In the symmetric case $\mathcal H\otimes\mathbb Q$ is a free supercommutative algebra generated by $V = V^{\mathrm{prim}}\otimes \mathbb Q[x]$, $x$ an even variable of bidegree $(0,2)$, with $V^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\gamma,k}\ne0$ for only finitely many $k$ at each $\gamma$. For one vertex, this is equivalent to the sign property $(-1)^{m-1}\delta^{(d)}(n,m)\ge 0$.

### 1.3 Motivic DT invariants via Sym (KS §6.2)

Motivic DT series (KS §5.3): $A = \sum_\gamma (-\mathbb L^{1/2})^{-\dim M_\gamma/G_\gamma}\,[H^\bullet_{G_\gamma}(M_\gamma,W_\gamma)]\,\hat e_\gamma$; for zero potential $-\dim M_\gamma/G_\gamma = \chi_Q(\gamma,\gamma)$.

**Theorem 10 (KS §6.2).** For any sector $V\subset\mathbb H_+$ the generating series $A_V$ is quantum admissible. (Admissible in one variable: $F = \prod_{n\ge1}\prod_{i\in\mathbb Z}(q^{i/2}x^n;q)_\infty^{c(n,i)}$, $c(n,i)\in\mathbb Z$, finitely many $i$ per $n$.)

**Corollary 3 + Definition 21 (KS §6.2) — the definition of $\Omega^{\mathrm{mot}}(\gamma)$.** If $\chi_R$ restricted to $\Gamma_l = Z^{-1}(l)$ is symmetric, there exist $\Omega^{\mathrm{mot}}(\gamma)\in K_0(\mathcal C)$ with

$$\sum_{\gamma\in Z^{-1}(l)}[\mathcal H^{\mathrm{mod}}_{l,\gamma}]\,x^\gamma \;=\; \operatorname{Sym}\!\left(\sum_{\gamma\in Z^{-1}(l)} \Omega^{\mathrm{mot}}(\gamma)\cdot[H^\bullet(\mathbb P^\infty)]\cdot x^\gamma\right),$$

$[H^\bullet(\mathbb P^\infty)] = 1+q+q^2+\cdots = (1-q)^{-1}$. $\Omega^{\mathrm{mot}}(\gamma)$ is the **motivic DT invariant**; its image under $K_0(\mathcal C)\to\mathbb Z[q^{\pm1/2}]$ is the **refined (quantum) DT invariant**, and evaluation at $q^{1/2}=1$ gives numerical DT invariants. For symmetric $Q$ with trivial stability, the single slope contains everything, so this is the plethystic-$\operatorname{Sym}$ factorization of $P_Q$ itself.

## 2. Efimov: positivity (arXiv:1103.2736)

A. I. Efimov, *Cohomological Hall algebra of a symmetric quiver*, arXiv:1103.2736, Compositio Math. 148 (2012) 1133–1146.

Grading convention: $H^k_{G_\gamma}(M_\gamma)$ is placed in bidegree $(\gamma,\, k+\chi_Q(\gamma,\gamma))$ (equivalent to KS §2.6). Parity induced by the $\mathbb Z$-grading.

**Theorem 1.1 (freeness).** For any finite symmetric quiver $Q$, the $(\mathbb Z^I_{\ge0}\times\mathbb Z)$-graded algebra $(\mathcal H,\star)$ is a free supercommutative algebra generated by a graded vector space $V = V^{\mathrm{prim}}\otimes\mathbb Q[x]$, $x$ of bidegree $(0,2)$, and for each $\gamma$ the space $V^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\gamma,k}$ is nonzero (finite-dimensional) for only finitely many $k$.

**Theorem 1.2 (degree bounds).** Put

$$N_\gamma(Q) := \frac12\Bigl(\sum_{i\ne j}a_{ij}\gamma_i\gamma_j + \sum_i \max(a_{ii}-1,0)\,\gamma_i(\gamma_i-1)\Bigr) - \sum_i\gamma_i + 2.$$

If $V^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\gamma,k}\ne0$ then $\gamma\ne0$, $k\equiv\chi_Q(\gamma,\gamma)\bmod 2$, and $\chi_Q(\gamma,\gamma)\le k < \chi_Q(\gamma,\gamma)+2N_\gamma(Q)$.

**Generating function (Efimov §4, eq. (4.1) — exact convention).**

$$\mathbb H_Q(\{t_i\},q) := \sum_{\gamma,k}(-1)^k\dim(\mathcal H_{\gamma,k})\,t^\gamma q^{k/2} = \sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb Z^I_{\ge0}}\frac{(-q^{1/2})^{\chi_Q(\gamma,\gamma)}}{\prod_{i\in I}(1-q)(1-q^2)\cdots(1-q^{\gamma_i})}\,t^\gamma \in \mathbb Z((q^{1/2}))[[\{t_i\}]].$$

**Corollary 4.1 (positivity).** $\mathbb H_Q = \prod_{\gamma,k}\bigl(q^{k/2}t^\gamma;q\bigr)_\infty^{(-1)^{k-1}c_{\gamma,k}}$ with $c_{\gamma,k} = \dim V^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\gamma,k} \ge 0$; $c_{\gamma,k}\ne0$ forces $\gamma\ne0$, $k\equiv\chi_Q(\gamma,\gamma)\bmod2$, $\chi_Q(\gamma,\gamma)\le k<\chi_Q(\gamma,\gamma)+2N_\gamma(Q)$.

**Quantum DT invariants (Efimov §4).** $\Omega(\gamma)(q) := \sum_k c_{\gamma,k}\,q^{k/2}\in\mathbb Z[q^{\pm1/2}]$, and

$$\Omega(\gamma)(q) = q^{\chi_Q(\gamma,\gamma)/2}\,\tilde\Omega(\gamma)(q),\qquad \tilde\Omega(\gamma)\in\mathbb N[q],\ \ \tilde\Omega(\gamma)(0)=1,\ \ \deg\tilde\Omega(\gamma) < N_\gamma(Q).$$

**Corollary 4.2 ($m$-loop translation).** With Reineke's $H_m(q,t) = \sum_n q^{(m-1)\binom n2}t^n/\prod_{i=1}^n(1-q^{-i})$ one has $H_m(q,t) = \mathbb H_{Q_m}\bigl((-1)^{m-1}t\,q^{(1-m)/2},\, q^{-1}\bigr)$ (note $q\to q^{-1}$ and the $t$-rescaling), $N_n(Q_m) = (m-1)\binom n2 - n + 2$, and

$$H_m(q,(-1)^{m-1}t) = \prod_{n\ge1,\,k\in\mathbb Z}\bigl(q^k t^n;q^{-1}\bigr)_\infty^{-(-1)^{(m-1)n}d_{n,k}},\qquad d_{n,k}\in\mathbb N,\quad d_{n,k}>0 \Rightarrow n-1\le k\le (m-1)\tbinom n2 .$$

Consequently **$\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n(q) = \sum_k d_{n,k}q^k$ is a monic polynomial of degree $(m-1)\binom n2$, divisible by $q^{\,n-1}$, with nonnegative coefficients.** (This pins down the normalization of Reineke's quantized invariants; see §4.6.) Efimov also notes the positivity was used by Mozgovoy (arXiv:1103.2100) to prove Kac's conjecture for quivers with sufficiently many loops.

## 3. Meinhardt–Reineke: integrality and intersection cohomology (arXiv:1411.4062)

S. Meinhardt, M. Reineke, *Donaldson–Thomas invariants versus intersection cohomology of quiver moduli*, arXiv:1411.4062, J. reine angew. Math. 754 (2019) 143–178.

**Theorem 1.1.** For a generic stability condition the DT "function" is the class of the intersection complex $IC_{\overline{M^{st}}}(\mathbb Q)$ of the closure of the stable locus in the coarse moduli space $M^{ss}$; in particular for every dimension vector $d$:

$$\mathrm{DT}_d = \begin{cases} IC_c(M^{ss}_d,\mathbb Q) = IC(M^{ss}_d,\mathbb Q)^\vee & \text{if } M^{st}_d\ne\emptyset,\\ 0 & \text{otherwise},\end{cases}$$

in the Grothendieck ring of (polarizable) mixed Hodge structures.

**Corollary 1.2 (positivity, acyclic case).** For $Q$ acyclic and generic stability, $\mathrm{DT}_d$ is a palindromic polynomial in $\mathbb L$ with positive coefficients.

**Theorem 1.4 (virtual smallness).** The Hilbert–Chow morphism $\pi: M^{ss}_{f,d}\to M^{ss}_d$ from framed to unframed moduli is projective and virtually small.

**Theorem 1.5 / Corollary 1.6 (integrality conjecture).** For generic stability, $\mathrm{DT}^{\mathrm{mot}}_d$ lies in the image of $K_0(\mathrm{Var}/k)[\mathbb L^{-1/2}] \to K_0(\mathrm{Var}/k)[\mathbb L^{-1/2},(\mathbb L^r-1)^{-1}: r\ge1]$ — i.e., no denominators. (Extends Efimov's symmetric-quiver case to arbitrary quivers and generic stability; relative version at every point of $M^{ss}$.)

**Theorem 4.7 (explicit $m$-loop application; exact statement).** Let $Q^{(m)}$ be the $m$-loop quiver, $m\ge2$, trivial stability. Then $M^{ss}_d(Q^{(m)}) = M^{(m)}_d := \mathrm{End}(\mathbb C^d)^m/\!\!/ GL_d(\mathbb C)$ (classical space of matrix invariants, irreducible normal affine of dimension $(m-1)d^2+1$). Let $U_d$ be the set of sequences $(a_1,\dots,a_d)\in\mathbb N^d$ with $\sum_i a_i = (m-1)d$, with the cyclic group $C_d$ acting by rotation. Call $a_*$ *primitive* if it differs from all proper rotations, and *almost primitive* if it is primitive, or $m$ is even, $d\equiv2\bmod4$, and $a_*$ is twice a primitive sequence of length $d/2$. Define $\deg(a_*) = \sum_{i=1}^d (d-i)a_i$ and $\deg C = $ minimal degree in the cyclic class $C$. Then

$$\sum_p \dim IC^p_c\bigl(M^{(m)}_d,\mathbb Q\bigr)\,v^p \;=\; v^{(m-1)d^2+1}\,\frac{1-v^{-2}}{1-v^{-2d}}\sum_{C\in U^{ap}_d/C_d} v^{-2\deg C}.$$

## 4. Reineke: the $m$-loop quiver (arXiv:1102.3978) — THE KEY SOURCE

M. Reineke, *Degenerate Cohomological Hall algebra and quantized Donaldson–Thomas invariants for $m$-loop quivers*, arXiv:1102.3978, Doc. Math. 17 (2012) 1–22.

### 4.1 Conventions

Noncommutative Hilbert scheme $\mathrm{Hilb}^{(m)}_n$ = stable ($v$ cyclic) tuples $(\phi_1,\dots,\phi_m,v)\in\mathrm{End}(\mathbb C^n)^m\oplus\mathbb C^n$ mod $GL_n$; parametrizes codimension-$n$ left ideals of $F^{(m)} = \mathbb C\langle x_1,\dots,x_m\rangle$. It has a cell decomposition with cells $Z_T$ indexed by $m$-ary trees $T$ with $n$ nodes, $\dim Z_T = d(T)$ [math/0306185; Theorem 2.1 here].

$$F(q,t) = \sum_{n\ge0} q^{(m-1)\binom n2}\sum_k \dim H^k(\mathrm{Hilb}^{(m)}_n)\,q^{-k/2}\,t^n \in \mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}][[t]],\qquad F(t) := F(1,t) = \sum_n \chi(\mathrm{Hilb}^{(m)}_n)t^n,$$

$$H(q,t) = \sum_{n\ge0}\frac{q^{(m-1)\binom n2}}{(1-q^{-1})(1-q^{-2})\cdots(1-q^{-n})}\,t^n \in\mathbb Q(q)[[t]].$$

**Corollary 2.2.** (1) $F(q,t) = 1 + t\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}F(q,q^k t)$ (q-difference equation from tree grafting). (2) $F(t) = 1+tF(t)^m$. (3) $\chi(\mathrm{Hilb}^{(m)}_n) = \frac{1}{(m-1)n+1}\binom{mn}{n}$ (Fuss–Catalan). (4) **Framed/unframed product formula:** $F(q,t) = \dfrac{H(q,t)}{H(q,q^{-1}t)}$ (special case of Engel–Reineke, arXiv:0706.4306, Thm 5.2).

### 4.2 Numerical DT invariants (Definition 3.1, Theorem 3.2)

**Definition 3.1.** $\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n\in\mathbb Q$ defined by

$$F\bigl((-1)^{m-1}t\bigr) = \prod_{n\ge1}\bigl(1-t^n\bigr)^{-(-1)^{(m-1)n}\, n\,\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n}.$$

**Theorem 3.2** (proved in arXiv:0903.0261). $\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n\in\mathbb N$, explicitly:

$$\boxed{\ \mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n = \frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{d|n}\mu\!\left(\frac nd\right)(-1)^{(m-1)(n-d)}\binom{md-1}{d-1}\ }$$

(the binomial argument is $(md-1, d-1)$ with $d$ the summation variable; confirmed against Reineke's survey arXiv:2410.03219, Example 2.4(5), and verified numerically). Examples as printed in the paper:

$$\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_1 = 1,\quad \mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_2 = \left\lfloor \frac m2\right\rfloor,\quad \mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_3 = \frac{m(m-1)}{2},\quad \mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_4 = \frac{m(m-1)(2m-1)}{3},\quad \mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_5 = \frac{5m(m-1)(5m^2-5m+2)}{24},$$

$$\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_6 = \frac{m(m-1)\bigl(36m^3-54m^2+31m-\frac{13+5(-1)^{m-1}}{2}\bigr)}{20},\qquad \mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_7 = \frac{7m(m-1)(343m^4-686m^3+539m^2-196m+36)}{720}.$$

*(Caution: the PDF text layer garbles the $\mathrm{DT}_6$ constant term; the form above is reconstructed to match Theorem 3.2 — checked: $m=2\Rightarrow13$, $m=3\Rightarrow171$.)* $\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n$ is a polynomial in $m$ except when $n\equiv2\bmod4$. Leading term $\frac{n^{n-2}}{n!}m^{n-1}$ as a function of $m$.

### 4.3 Quantized DT invariants (Conjecture 3.3 route; proved via §§5–6)

**Conjecture 3.3** (= KS Conjecture 1 variant; established by the paper's Theorem 6.8 modulo positivity, and by Efimov in full): there is a product expansion

$$H\bigl(q,(-1)^{m-1}t\bigr) = \prod_{n\ge1}\prod_{k\ge0}\prod_{l\ge0}\bigl(1-q^{k-l}t^n\bigr)^{-(-1)^{(m-1)n}c_{n,k}},\qquad c_{n,k}\in\mathbb N \text{ (finitely many per }n).$$

Setting $\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n(q) = \sum_{k\ge0}c_{n,k}q^k$: it is a polynomial with nonnegative coefficients and $\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n(1) = \mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n$. In $\lambda$-ring form ($\operatorname{Exp} = \exp\circ\sum_{i\ge1}\psi_i/i$, $\psi_i(q)=q^i$, $\psi_i(t)=t^i$; Lemma 8.3: $\operatorname{Exp}(\sum c_{i,k}q^kt^i) = \prod(1-q^kt^i)^{-c_{i,k}}$):

$$H\bigl(q,(-1)^{m-1}t\bigr) = \operatorname{Exp}\!\left(\frac{1}{1-q^{-1}}\sum_{n\ge1}\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n(q)\,\bigl((-1)^{m-1}t\bigr)^n\right),$$

where $((-1)^{m-1}t)^n$ means the coefficient sign twist $(-1)^{(m-1)n}t^n$ (the $\lambda$-structure acts on plain $t$).

### 4.4 CoHA and degenerate CoHA

**Theorem 4.1/4.3** (from KS): $\mathcal H = \bigoplus_n H^\bullet_{GL_n}(\mathrm{End}(\mathbb C^n)^m)$, bidegree of $H^k$: $(n, (m-1)\binom n2 - k/2)$; shuffle model on $\bigoplus_n\mathbb Q[x_1,\dots,x_n]^{S_n}$ with kernel $\prod\prod(x_{j_l}-x_{i_k})^{m-1}$; a degree-$k$ symmetric function in $n$ variables has bidegree $(n,(m-1)\binom n2-k)$. Poincaré–Hilbert series of $\mathcal H$ equals $H(q,t)$.

**Definition 5.1–5.2 (degenerate CoHA).** The quantized shuffle with kernel $\prod\prod(x_{j_l}-q\,x_{i_k})^{m-1}$ at $q=0$ degenerates to the algebra $A = \bigoplus\mathbb Q\lambda$ on partitions with product $\mu*\nu = \mu\cup S^{(m-1)\ell(\nu)}\mu$… (explicitly: append $\nu$ shifted by $(m-1)\ell(\mu)$ — see Lemma 5.4: for $\lambda,\mu\in T$, $\lambda*\mu$ = concatenate $\lambda$ with $S^{(m-1)\ell(\lambda)}\mu$). Let $T_n$ = partitions $0\le\lambda_1\le\cdots\le\lambda_n$ with $\lambda_i\le(m-1)(i-1)$, weight $\mathrm{wt}(\lambda) = (m-1)\binom n2 - |\lambda|$. $T^0$ = those with $\lambda_i<(m-1)(i-1)$ for $i\ge2$; $B \cong T(B^0)$ (tensor algebra), Lyndon words in the alphabet $T^0$ give $T^L$, and (Theorem 5.9) $A \cong \operatorname{Sym}\bigl(\bigoplus_{i\ge0}S^iB^L\bigr)$.

**Corollary 5.10.** $H(q,t) = \operatorname{Exp}\Bigl(\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{1}{1-q^{-n}}\,Q_n(q)\,t^n\Bigr)$, $Q_n(q) = \sum_{\lambda\in T^L_n}q^{\mathrm{wt}(\lambda)}$.

**Theorem 5.11 (signed version).** With $T^{L,+} = T^L$ for $m$ odd and $T^{L,+} = T^L\cup\{\lambda*\lambda\,:\,\lambda\in T^L,\ \ell(\lambda)\text{ odd}\}$ for $m$ even, and $\bar Q_n(q) = \sum_{\lambda\in T^{L,+}_n}q^{\mathrm{wt}(\lambda)}$:

$$H\bigl(q,(-1)^{m-1}t\bigr) = \operatorname{Exp}\!\left(\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{1}{1-q^{-n}}\,\bar Q_n(q)\,\bigl((-1)^{m-1}t\bigr)^n\right).$$

**Corollary 5.12.** $\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n = \frac1n\,|T^{L,+}_n|$.

*Worked example printed in the paper ($m=2$, $n=4$):* $T_4$ has 14 elements; $T^L_4 = \{(0000),(0001),(0002),(0003),(0011),(0012),(0013),(0023)\}$ (8 elements).

### 4.5 Cyclic-sequence model (§6)

$U_n$ = sequences $(a_1,\dots,a_n)\in\mathbb N^n$ with $\sum a_i = (m-1)n$; $C_n$ acts by rotation; $U^{\mathrm{prim}}_n$ = sequences distinct from all proper rotations; $U^{\mathrm{prim},+}_n = U^{\mathrm{prim}}_n\cup U^{\bar n\text{-prim}}_n$ (adding $(n/2)$-fold-primitive doubles) iff $m$ even and $n\equiv2\bmod4$, else $U^{\mathrm{prim}}_n$. The map $\varphi(\lambda) = (\lambda_2-\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n-\lambda_{n-1},(m-1)n-\lambda_n)$ is a bijection $T^{L,(+)}_n\to U^{\mathrm{prim},(+)}_n/C_n$ (Prop. 6.3, Cor. 6.4). The weight transported to sequences is

$$\mathrm{wt}(a_1,\dots,a_n) = \sum_{i=1}^n (n-i)\,\bigl(m-1-a_i\bigr),$$

which, mod $n$, is rotation-invariant, and attains its maximum on each cyclic class at an "admissible" element ($\sum_{j\le i}a_j\le(m-1)i\ \forall i$) — Lemma 6.5. Set $\mathrm{wt}(C) := \max_{a\in C}\mathrm{wt}(a)$, so $\bar Q_n(q) = \sum_{C\in U^{\mathrm{prim},+}_n/C_n}q^{\mathrm{wt}(C)}$. (Note: $\mathrm{wt}(a) = (m-1)\binom n2-\deg(a)$ with $\deg$ as in §3/Theorem 4.7 of Meinhardt–Reineke; max-weight = min-degree.)

**Theorem 6.7.** $\bar Q_n(q)$ is divisible by $[n] := 1+q+\cdots+q^{n-1}$ and $\frac1{[n]}\bar Q_n(q)\in\mathbb Z[q]$. Root-of-unity evaluation used in the proof: $P_n(q) := \sum_{a\in U_n}q^{\mathrm{wt}(a)}$ is the $t^{(m-1)n}$-coefficient of $q^{(m-1)\binom n2}/\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(1-q^{-i}t)$, and $P_n(\zeta_n^s) = (-1)^{(m-1)(n-1)s}\binom{m\gcd(s,n)-1}{\gcd(s,n)-1}$; $q$-Möbius inversion gives $\bar Q_n(q) \equiv \frac1n\sum_{d|n}\mu(n/d)P_d(q^{n/d}) \bmod (q^n-1)$.

### 4.6 Theorem 6.8 — the MAIN refined formula (with a normalization caveat)

**Theorem 6.8 as printed** (arXiv v1 text layer; also quoted this way in Kucharski–Sułkowski arXiv:1608.06600, eq. (B.4)):

$$\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n(q) \;=\; q^{1-n}\,\frac{1}{[n]}\sum_{C\in U^{\mathrm{prim},+}_n/C_n} q^{\mathrm{wt}(C)},$$

and: for any $i=0,\dots,n-1$, the numerical $\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n$ equals the number of classes $C\in U^{\mathrm{prim},+}_n/C_n$ with $\mathrm{wt}(C)\equiv i\bmod n$.

**Caveat [verified computation].** The prefactor $q^{1-n}$ is inconsistent with the paper's own Definition of $\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n(q)$ in §3 (Conjecture 3.3 normalization) and with Efimov's Corollary 4.2. Comparing Theorem 5.11 with the §3 Exp-form termwise gives $\frac{\mathrm{DT}_n(q)}{1-q^{-1}} = \frac{\bar Q_n(q)}{1-q^{-n}}$, i.e.

$$\boxed{\ \mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n(q) \;=\; q^{\,n-1}\,\frac{1}{[n]}\sum_{C\in U^{\mathrm{prim},+}_n/C_n} q^{\mathrm{wt}(C)}\ }\qquad\Bigl(\tfrac{1-q^{-1}}{1-q^{-n}} = \tfrac{q^{n-1}}{[n]}\Bigr).$$

Three independent confirmations of $q^{n-1}$: (i) symbolic expansion of $H(q,(-1)^{m-1}t)$ and plethystic Log through $t^6$ for $m=2$ and $t^5$ for $m=3$ reproduces exactly $q^{n-1}\bar Q_n/[n]$, never $q^{1-n}\bar Q_n/[n]$; (ii) Efimov Cor. 4.2: $\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n(q)$ is monic of degree $(m-1)\binom n2$ and divisible by $q^{n-1}$ — satisfied by the $q^{n-1}$ version only (e.g. $m=2$, $n=2$: $q^{n-1}$ gives $q$; $q^{1-n}$ gives $q^{-1}$); (iii) consistency with Meinhardt–Reineke Theorem 4.7. The two versions differ by $q^{2(n-1)}$; at $q=1$ they agree, so all numerical statements are unaffected. When implementing, use $q^{n-1}$ (or state the convention being matched).

### 4.7 Relation to Higgs moduli (§7)

**Theorem 7.1.** For $\gcd(d,n)=1$, $\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n = |H_{n,d}|$, where $H_{n,d}$ = sequences $(l_1,\dots,l_n)\in\mathbb Z^n$ with $l_{k+1}-l_k+(m-1)\ge0$, $\sum l_i = d$, $\frac1k\sum_{i\le k}l_i\ge\frac dn$ for $k<n$ — type-$(1,\dots,1)$ $\mathbb C^*$-fixed points on $SL_n$-Higgs moduli (Hausel–Thaddeus; Hausel–Rodriguez-Villegas connection, "score sequences of complete tournaments" for $d\equiv0$).

## 5. Reineke: noncommutative Hilbert schemes (arXiv:math/0306185) and the integrality engine (arXiv:0903.0261)

### 5.1 arXiv:math/0306185

M. Reineke, *Cohomology of noncommutative Hilbert schemes*, arXiv:math/0306185, Algebr. Represent. Theory 8 (2005) 541–561.

$H^{(m)}_{d,n}$ ($=\mathrm{Hilb}^{(m)}_{d,n}$): stable points of $\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb C^n,\mathbb C^d)\oplus\mathrm{End}(\mathbb C^d)^m$ mod $GL_d$; smooth irreducible of dimension $nd+(m-1)d^2$; parametrizes codim-$d$ subrepresentations of the free module $A^n$, $A = k\langle x_1,\dots,x_m\rangle$; equivalently framed stable moduli of the $m$-loop quiver with framing vector $n$.

**Theorem 1.3.** $H^{(m)}_{d,n}$ has a cell decomposition with cells parametrized by $m$-ary forests with $n$ roots and $d$ nodes.

**Theorem 1.4 (exact convention).** Define

$$\zeta^{(m)}_n(q,t) = \sum_{d=0}^\infty q^{(m-1)\frac{d(d-1)}2+(n-1)d}\sum_k\dim H^k\bigl(H^{(m)}_{d,n}\bigr)\,q^{-k/2}\,t^d \in\mathbb Q[q][[t]].$$

Then $\zeta^{(m)}_n$ is determined by the functional equations

$$\zeta^{(m)}_n(q,t) = \prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\zeta^{(m)}_1(q,q^it),\qquad\qquad \zeta^{(m)}_1(q,t) = 1+t\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}\zeta^{(m)}_1(q,q^it).$$

**Euler characteristics (Corollary 4.5; the Fuss–Catalan formula).**

$$\boxed{\ \chi\bigl(H^{(m)}_{d,n}\bigr) = \frac{n}{(m-1)d+n}\binom{md+n-1}{d}\ }\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \chi\bigl(\mathrm{Hilb}^{(m)}_{d,1}\bigr) = \frac{1}{(m-1)d+1}\binom{md}{d}.$$

(Proof: bijection of $m$-ary forests with "plane forests of type $((m-1)d+n,0,\dots,0,d)$" + Stanley's enumeration Thm 5.3.10. For $m=2,n=1$: Catalan numbers.)

**Corollary 4.4 (Poincaré polynomials).** $\sum_i\dim H^i(H^{(m)}_{d,n})t^i = \sum_{S_*\in F^{(m)}_{d,n}}t^{2((m-1)d^2+nd-d(S_*))}$, sum over $m$-ary forests, $d(S_*)$ a combinatorial dimension statistic. At $q=1$ the specialization satisfies $\zeta^{(m)}_1(1,t) = 1+t\,\zeta^{(m)}_1(1,t)^m$, and for $m=2$, $\zeta^{(2)}_1(1,t) = \frac{1-\sqrt{1-4t}}{2t}$ (Catalan GF); a continued-fraction expansion holds for $\zeta^{(2)}_1(q,t)$. The series has its singularity at $t = \frac{(m-1)^{m-1}}{m^m}$.

**Theorem 1.5 (asymptotics).** After suitable renormalization, the distribution of Betti numbers of $H^{(m)}_{d,1}$ for $d\to\infty$ has the **Airy distribution** as limit law.

### 5.2 arXiv:0903.0261 (integrality engine)

M. Reineke, *Cohomology of quiver moduli, functional equations, and integrality of Donaldson–Thomas type invariants*, arXiv:0903.0261, Compositio Math. 147 (2011) 943–964.

**Theorem 4.2 / Corollary 4.3 (framed factorization, general quiver, numerical).** For smooth models ("framed moduli") $M^\Theta_{d,n}(Q)$ and stable moduli:

$$\sum_{d\in\Lambda^+_\mu}\chi\bigl(M^\Theta_{d,n}(Q)\bigr)t^d = \prod_{d\in{}'\Lambda^+_\mu} R_d(t)^{\chi(M^{st}_d(Q))\cdot(n\cdot d)},\qquad R_d(t) = \Bigl(1-t^d S^d_\mu(t)\Bigr)^{-1},\qquad S^d_\mu(t) = \prod_{e}\bigl(1-t^eS^e_\mu(t)\bigr)^{-\langle d,e\rangle\chi(M^{st}_e(Q))}.$$

**Theorem 5.1/5.9 (arithmetic duality for Euler products)** — the mechanism behind integrality of $\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n$: for $F(t) = \prod_{i\ge1}(1-(-t)^i)^{-ia_i}$ vs. algebraic functional equations, integrality of one side propagates to the other ("the choice of signs is essential"). **Theorem 6.3** confirms the KS integrality conjecture (Kontsevich–Soibelman arXiv:0811.2435, Conj. 1) for the factorization $T^{(m)}_{1,0}T^{(m)}_{0,1} = \prod^{\leftarrow}(T^{(m)}_{a,b})^{d(a,b,m)}$, $d(a,b,m)\in\mathbb Z$ (Poisson automorphisms/generalized Kronecker quivers).

## 6. Framing / wall-crossing formula for framed objects

### 6.1 Mozgovoy (arXiv:1104.4335)

S. Mozgovoy, *Wall-crossing formulas for framed objects*, arXiv:1104.4335, Q. J. Math. 64 (2013) 489–513.

Setup: framed objects $(M,\mathbb C)$ over quiver $Q' = Q + $ framing vertex with $w_i$ arrows to $i\in Q_0$; $\Gamma = \Gamma^0\oplus\mathbb Z$, framing rank $v$; motivic quantum torus $x^\alpha\circ x^\beta = (-q^{1/2})^{\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle}x^{\alpha+\beta}$; twist operators $S_\lambda: x^\alpha\mapsto(-q^{1/2})^{\lambda(\alpha)}x^\alpha$, $\nu(\alpha) := \langle\alpha,(0,1)\rangle$ (for quivers, $\nu(\alpha) = w\cdot\alpha$ up to convention).

**Theorem 4.5 (Hall-algebra wall-crossing).** $\tilde A_{c,\mu} = \tilde B_\mu\,\tilde A_{c^-,\mu} = \tilde A_{c^+,\mu}\,\tilde B_\mu$.

**Theorem 4.11 (quantum-torus wall-crossing).** $A_{c,\mu} = S_\nu B_\mu\circ A_{c^-,\mu} = A_{c^+,\mu}\circ S_{-\nu}B_\mu$.

**Theorem 5.2 (quivers; framed vs. unframed, refined).** For any stability $\theta\in\mathbb R^{Q_0}$, slope $\mu$, framing $w\in\mathbb N^{Q_0}$, with $B^\theta_\mu = \sum_{\mu(\alpha)=\mu}(-\mathbb L^{1/2})^{T(\alpha)}[\mathfrak M^\theta_\alpha]x^\alpha$ (stack invariants) and $\mathcal M^\theta_{\alpha,w}$ the smooth model (framed stable moduli):

$$\sum_{\mu(\alpha)=\mu}(-\mathbb L^{1/2})^{T(\alpha)}\,[\mathcal M^\theta_{\alpha,w}]\,x^\alpha \;=\; \frac{1}{\mathbb L-1}\;S_{2\nu}B^\theta_\mu\circ\bigl(B^\theta_\mu\bigr)^{-1}.$$

**Proposition 5.7 (NCDT version, trivial stability).** $A^w_{NCDT} = S_{2\nu}B_U\circ B_U^{-1}$ where $B_U$ = universal (trivial-stability) DT series. For a **symmetric** quiver the quantum torus is commutative after the sign twist and $S_{2\nu}$ is the substitution $x_i\mapsto q^{w_i}x_i$ (up to sign conventions), recovering Engel–Reineke: framed series $=\ \frac{P(q^{w\cdot}x)}{P(x)}$-type ratio. For the $m$-loop quiver with 1-framing this is exactly $F(q,t) = H(q,t)/H(q,q^{-1}t)$ of §4.1.

### 6.2 Refined 1-framing formula in DT-variables (KRSS convention)

Kucharski–Reineke–Stošić–Sułkowski (arXiv:1707.04017), eqs. (3.6)–(3.7): for a symmetric quiver and framing vector $n\in\mathbb N^{Q_0}$, with $P_Q$ and $\Omega_d(q)$ as in §8.1 below:

$$\frac{P_Q\bigl((-q)^{n}x\bigr)}{P_Q\bigl((-q)^{-n}x\bigr)} = \operatorname{Exp}\!\left(\sum_{d\ne0}\frac{(-q)^{n\cdot d}-(-q)^{-n\cdot d}}{(-q)-(-q)^{-1}}\,\Omega_d(q)\,x^d\right)\quad\xrightarrow{\ q\to1\ }\quad \sum_d \chi\bigl(\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,n}(Q)\bigr)x^d = \prod_{d\ne0}\bigl(1-x^d\bigr)^{-(n\cdot d)\,\Omega_d(1)}.$$

This is the exact refined statement of "framed generating series = wall-crossing transform of unframed DT invariants" in the knots–quivers normalization; the $q\to1$ limit is attributed to Reineke [their ref. 49].

## 7. Explicit formulas valid for all $d$; words/Lyndon models; asymptotics

### 7.1 What exists (summary of provenance)

1. **Numerical (unrefined), $m$-loop, closed form:** Theorem 3.2 of arXiv:1102.3978 (§4.2 above) — Möbius/binomial formula. Equivalent counts: Lyndon-type classes ($\frac1n|T^{L,+}_n|$), cyclic classes of primitive sequences, Higgs-type sequences $H_{n,d}$, and (arXiv:2207.11861) $S_\gamma$-orbits of break divisors.
2. **Refined, $m$-loop, closed combinatorial form:** Theorem 6.8 of arXiv:1102.3978 (as corrected in §4.6): $\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n(q) = \frac{q^{n-1}}{[n]}\sum_{C}q^{\mathrm{wt}(C)}$ over cyclic classes of (almost-)primitive sequences. **No closed product/hypergeometric formula for the refined $\Omega_n(q)$ (all $n$, all $m$) is known** — the state of the art is this polynomial-time combinatorial sum (plus the structural interpretations of §8).
3. **Numerical, arbitrary symmetric quiver, closed form:** Panfil–Stošić–Sułkowski (arXiv:1802.04573), Props. 3.2–3.3 (below).
4. **Refined, arbitrary symmetric quiver:** algorithmic only (plethystic inversion of $P_Q$); structural results (§8) give positivity, IC/Chow meaning, generating algebras, and diagonalization to infinite loop quivers, but no closed form.

### 7.2 Words/Lyndon model — Kucharski–Sułkowski (arXiv:1608.06600)

P. Kucharski, P. Sułkowski, *BPS counting for knots and combinatorics on words*, arXiv:1608.06600, JHEP 11 (2016) 120.

Extremal colored HOMFLY-PT of the framed unknot/twist knots (their eq. (4.8)):

$$P^m_r(q) = (-1)^{rm}\,\frac{q^{r^2m - r(m-1)}}{(q^2;q^2)_r}$$

(the $m$-loop quiver series in the variable $q_{\mathrm{here}}^2 = q_{\mathrm{KS}}$). Its ratio $Y^m(x,q) = \frac{P^m(q^2x,q)}{P^m(x,q)}$ satisfies the **quantum quiver A-polynomial**

$$\hat A^m(\hat x,\hat y,q) = 1-\hat y^2 - q\hat x\,(-\hat y^2)^m \qquad (\hat y\hat x = q\hat x\hat y \text{ conventions of their §2}),$$

giving the recursion $\bigl(1-q^{2(r+1)}\bigr)P^m_{r+1}(q) = (-1)^m q^{2rm+1}P^m_r(q)$. The LMOV/BPS invariants are extracted by a Lyndon-word construction on "maximally-$2(m-1)$-step lists" $\varphi$ ($\varphi_1 = 1$, $\varphi_i\ge1$, $\varphi_{i+1}-\varphi_i\in2\mathbb Z$, $\varphi_{i+1}-\varphi_i\le 2(m-1)$), equivalent (their Appendix B, bijection $\varphi_i = 2((m-1)(i-1)-\lambda_i)+1$, $\mathrm{wt}(\varphi) = 2\,\mathrm{wt}^{R}(\lambda)+n$) to Reineke's degenerate-CoHA combinatorics:

$$N^m_r(q) = \sum_j N^m_{r,j}q^{j+1} = \frac{1}{[r]_{q^2}}\sum_{\varphi\in T^{L,+}_r}(-1)^{(m+1)r}q^{\mathrm{wt}(\varphi)},\qquad\quad N^m_r(q) = (-1)^{(m+1)r}\,q^{3r-2}\,\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_r(q^2)\ \ \text{(their (B.5), using (B.4) i.e. the }q^{1-r}\text{ form; see §4.6)}.$$

Integrality of these LMOV invariants for the framed unknot and twist knots follows from Reineke's theorem. For $m=2$ this produces a non-standard $q$-deformation of the Catalan numbers $C_n(q) = \sum_{k_0+k_1 = n-1}q^{2k_1+1}C_{k_0}(q)C_{k_1}(q)$, $C_n(q) = q^nc_n(q^2)$ (their §4.3, with Dyck-path area interpretation); general $m$: $q$-Fuss–Catalan deformations.

### 7.3 Closed formulas for arbitrary symmetric quivers (numerical) — Panfil–Stošić–Sułkowski (arXiv:1802.04573)

M. Panfil, M. Stošić, P. Sułkowski, *Donaldson–Thomas invariants, torus knots, and lattice paths*, arXiv:1802.04573, Phys. Rev. D 98 (2018) 026022.

For $C$ a symmetric $m\times m$ matrix, $y(x_1,\dots,x_m) = \lim_{q\to1}\frac{P_C(q^2x)}{P_C(x)} = \sum_l b_{l_1\dots l_m}x^l = \prod_{d\ne0}(1-x^d)^{\Omega_{d_1\dots d_m}}$ (their (3.17); note their $\Omega_d$ here is the exponent in this specific product — for one vertex it equals $-(-1)^{(m-1)n}n\,\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n$ in Reineke's Definition 3.1):

**Proposition 3.2.** $\displaystyle b_{l_1,\dots,l_m} = (-1)^{\sum_i(C_{i,i}+1)l_i}\,A(l_1,\dots,l_m)\prod_{j=1}^m\frac{1}{1+\sum_iC_{i,j}l_i}\binom{1+\sum_iC_{i,j}l_i}{l_j}$, where $A(l) = 1+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\sum_{\text{admissible }\Sigma_k}\prod_{(i_u,j_u)\in\Sigma_k}C_{i_u,j_u}l_{i_u}$ (admissible = no repeated targets, no cycles).

**Proposition 3.3.** $\displaystyle \log y(x) = \sum_{l\ne0}(-1)^{\sum_i(C_{i,i}+1)l_i}\,A^{\max}(l)\,\prod_{j=1}^m\frac{1}{\sum_iC_{i,j}l_i}\binom{\sum_iC_{i,j}l_i}{l_j}\,x^l$, $A^{\max}$ = top-degree part of $A$; numerical DT invariants follow by Möbius inversion of the log coefficients.

One vertex, $f$ loops ($C = [f]$): $b_i = \frac{(-1)^{(f+1)i}}{fi+1}\binom{fi+1}{i}$ — and $\frac{1}{fi+1}\binom{fi+1}{i} = \frac{1}{(f-1)i+1}\binom{fi}{i}$, the Fuss–Catalan number, recovering §5.1; $[x^i]\log y = (-1)^{(f+1)i}\frac{1}{fi}\binom{fi}{i}$.

**Quiver A-polynomials & lattice paths.** Classical generating functions of quivers of $(r,s)$ torus knots in framing $rs$ = generating functions of lattice paths under the line of slope $r/s$ (Prop. 4.1; quantum version Prop. 4.2 with $q$-weighting by area); these satisfy algebraic **extremal A-polynomial equations** (their §4.7, via Duchon grammars) — for the $f$-loop quiver the classical curve is $y = 1+(-1)^{f-1}xy^f$ (equivalently KS $P^{cl}$, §1.1). Asymptotics of DT/BPS invariants are controlled by the branch-point singularity of this curve; for the $m$-loop quiver the radius of convergence is $t_* = \frac{(m-1)^{m-1}}{m^m}$ (cf. §5.1), so $n\,\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n \sim \mathrm{const}\cdot n^{-3/2}\,\bigl(\tfrac{m^m}{(m-1)^{m-1}}\bigr)^n$; the refined Betti distributions obey the Airy law (math/0306185, Thm 1.5). See also Larraguível–Noshchenko–Panfil–Sułkowski, *Nahm sums, quiver A-polynomials and topological recursion*, arXiv:2004.10837 (JHEP 2020): quiver A-polynomials as quantum curves, topological recursion reproducing $\Omega_d$.

## 8. 2015–2026 structural and computational developments

### 8.1 Knots–quivers correspondence (the physics normalization of $\Omega_d(q)$)

P. Kucharski, M. Reineke, M. Stošić, P. Sułkowski, arXiv:1707.02991 (*BPS states, knots and quivers*, Phys. Rev. D 96 (2017) 121902(R)) and arXiv:1707.04017 (*Knots-quivers correspondence*, Adv. Theor. Math. Phys. 23 (2019) 1849–1902). Their conventions (1707.04017 §3), used across the physics literature:

$$\langle d,e\rangle_Q = \sum_i d_ie_i - \sum_{\alpha:i\to j}d_ie_j,\qquad P_Q(x) = \sum_{d\in\mathbb N^{Q_0}}(-q)^{-\langle d,d\rangle_Q}\,x^d\prod_{i\in Q_0}\prod_{j=1}^{d_i}\frac{1}{1-q^{-2j}},$$

$$P_Q(x) = \operatorname{Exp}\!\left(\frac{1}{q^{-1}-q}\sum_{d\ne0}\Omega_d(q)\,x^d\right),\qquad \Omega_d(q) = \sum_j\Omega_{d,j}(-q)^j,\qquad P_Q(x) = \prod_{d\ne0}\prod_{j\in\mathbb Z}\prod_{k\ge0}\bigl(1-(-1)^jx^dq^{j+2k+1}\bigr)^{-\Omega_{d,j}},$$

with $\Omega_{d,j}\in\mathbb N$ (Efimov), and interpretations: intersection Betti numbers of $M^{ss,\mathrm{simp}}_d$ / Chow–Betti numbers of $M^{\mathrm{simp}}_d$ (Meinhardt–Reineke; Franzen–Reineke). Here $q_{\mathrm{KRSS}}^2 = q_{\mathrm{FR}}$, i.e. their $q$ is a square root of the "cohomological" $q$. Main conjecture (theorem in many cases): generating series of colored HOMFLY-PT = $P_Q$ of an associated symmetric quiver under $x_i = x\,a^{a_i}q^{l_i-1}(-1)^{t_i}$; LMOV integrality follows from quiver DT integrality.

### 8.2 Franzen–Reineke: Chow-theoretic refinement (arXiv:1512.03748)

H. Franzen, M. Reineke, *Semi-stable Chow–Hall algebras of quivers and quantized Donaldson–Thomas invariants*, arXiv:1512.03748, Alg. Number Theory 12 (2018) 1001–1025. Convention: $A(q,t) = \sum_d(-q^{1/2})^{-\chi(d,d)}\prod_i\prod_{\nu=1}^{d_i}(1-q^{-\nu})^{-1}t^d$; Theorem 3.2 defines $\tilde\Omega^\theta_d(q) = \sum_k\tilde\Omega_{d,2k}q^k\in\mathbb Z[q]$ by $A^{\theta,\mu}(q^{-1},t) = \operatorname{Exp}\bigl(\frac{1}{1-q}\sum_d(-q^{1/2})^{\chi(d,d)}\tilde\Omega^\theta_d(q)t^d\bigr)$.

**Theorem 9.2.** For symmetric $Q$, stability $\theta$, $d\ne0$: $\Omega_{d,k} = \dim A_{1-\frac{k+\chi(d,d)}2}\bigl(M^{\mathrm{st}}_d\bigr)_{\mathbb Q}$ if $k\equiv\chi(d,d)\bmod2$ and $M^{\mathrm{st}}_d\ne\emptyset$, else $0$ — **quantized DT invariants are Chow–Betti numbers of moduli of simple representations**; nonzero only for $\chi(d,d)\le k\le 2-\chi(d,d)$ (recovering $N_d(Q)\le 1-\chi(d,d)$, the dimension of the moduli space). §10.4 treats multiple loop quivers via equivariant Chow rings $\mathrm{Sym}(X_d)^{S_d}$, DT values as in arXiv:1102.3978.

### 8.3 Meinhardt–Reineke era integrality → cohomological DT

Also relevant context: B. Davison, S. Meinhardt, *Cohomological Donaldson–Thomas theory of a quiver with potential and quantum enveloping algebras*, arXiv:1601.02479, Invent. Math. 221 (2020) 777–871 — categorified integrality: the CoHA is a free supercommutative algebra over the "BPS Lie algebra"; for symmetric quivers with zero potential this recovers and upgrades Efimov.

### 8.4 Reineke survey (arXiv:2410.03219) — the current canonical reference

M. Reineke, *Donaldson–Thomas invariants of symmetric quivers*, arXiv:2410.03219 (v2, revised 2026-07-06; for Proceedings of the Simons Semester "Knots, homologies and physics"). Convention: $P_A(x) = \sum_{d}\frac{(-q^{1/2})^{\sum_{i,j}(a_{ij}-\delta_{ij})d_id_j}}{\prod_i(1-q^{-1})\cdots(1-q^{-d_i})}x^d$ (i.e. $(-q^{1/2})^{-\chi(d,d)}$, Pochhammer in $q^{-1}$); factorization $P_A(x) = \prod_{d\ne0}\prod_{i\in\mathbb Z}\prod_{k\ge0}\bigl(1-q^{k+i+1/2}x^d\bigr)^{(-1)^{i+1}c_{d,i}}$; $\mathrm{DT}^A_d(q) = \sum_k c_{d,k}(-q^{1/2})^k$, normalized $\widetilde{\mathrm{DT}}{}^A_d(q)$ with **Theorem 2.3 (Efimov):** $\widetilde{\mathrm{DT}}{}^A_d(q)\in\mathbb N[-q^{1/2}]$. Catalogue of interpretations: **Theorem 4.3** (= MR intersection cohomology: $\widetilde{\mathrm{DT}}{}^A_d(q) = \sum_k\dim IH^k_c(M_d(Q^A))(-q^{1/2})^k$ if simples exist, else 0); **Theorem 4.5** (= FR Chow–Betti: $\widetilde{\mathrm{DT}}{}^A_d(q) = \sum_i\dim A_i(M^{\mathrm{simp}}_d)_{\mathbb Q}q^i$); **Theorem 5.4** (CoHA freeness); **Theorem 5.8** (Koszul-dual Lie superalgebra, §8.5); **Theorem 6.2** (break divisors, §8.6); **Theorem 6.3** (quiver diagonalization, §8.7). Worked examples: $A=[0]$: $\mathrm{DT}_1 = 1$, $\mathrm{DT}_{\ge2} = 0$; $A=[1]$ (one loop): $\mathrm{DT}_1(q) = -q^{1/2}$, $\mathrm{DT}_{\ge2} = 0$; $A_2$-quiver $\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]$: $\mathrm{DT}_{(1,0)} = \mathrm{DT}_{(0,1)} = 1$, $\mathrm{DT}_{(1,1)} = -q^{1/2}$, else 0; $\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}0&m\\m&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]$, $d = (1,k)$, $k\le m$: $\mathrm{DT}_d(q) = (-q^{1/2})^{k^2}\frac{(1-q)\cdots(1-q^m)}{(1-q)\cdots(1-q^k)\cdot(1-q)\cdots(1-q^{m-k})}$ (Gaussian binomial); $m$-loop: the boxed Möbius formula of §4.2.

### 8.5 Koszul algebras and vertex algebras

- V. Dotsenko, E. Feigin, M. Reineke, *Koszul algebras and Donaldson–Thomas invariants*, arXiv:2111.07588, Lett. Math. Phys. 112 (2022) no. 106. For each symmetric quiver, a supercommutative quadratic algebra $B(Q)$ whose Poincaré series is (essentially) $P_Q$; via Koszul duality with a Lie superalgebra $\mathfrak g^Q$, motivic DT invariants = Poincaré series of the Lie subalgebra $\ker(\partial_t)\subset\mathfrak g^Q$ — a new proof of positivity (numerical Koszulity always holds; Koszulity proved for some classes).
- V. Dotsenko, S. Mozgovoy, *DT invariants from vertex algebras*, arXiv:2108.10338, J. Inst. Math. Jussieu 24 (2025) 291–339. The graded dual of the CoHA of a symmetric quiver is the principal free vertex algebra associated to the Euler form; DT invariants acquire a vertex-algebra interpretation (universal enveloping vertex algebra of a vertex Lie algebra), yielding another positivity proof. (This, together with Mozgovoy's operadic formalism below, is the "Dotsenko–Mozgovoy" line.)
- V. Dotsenko, E. Feigin, P. Kucharski, M. Reineke, *Categorification of quiver diagonalization and Koszul algebras*, arXiv:2402.12768, Publ. RIMS 62 (2026) 379–396. Categorifies the diagonalization of §8.7 via Koszul algebras.

### 8.6 Zonotopal algebras / break divisors (combinatorics of $\Omega_d$)

M. Reineke, B. Rhoades, V. Tewari, *Zonotopal algebras, orbit harmonics, and Donaldson–Thomas invariants of symmetric quivers*, arXiv:2207.11861, IMRN 2023 no. 23, 20169–20210. For symmetric $Q$ with enough loops and dimension vector $\gamma$: quantum DT invariants = Hilbert series of the $S_\gamma$-invariant part of the Postnikov–Shapiro "slim subgraph" space (central zonotopal algebra of a graph $G_{Q,\gamma}$); numerical DT invariants = number of $S_\gamma$-orbits of **break divisors** on $G_{Q,\gamma}$ (survey Theorem 6.2: $\widetilde{\mathrm{DT}}{}^A_d(1) = \#\bigl(W_d\backslash\{\text{break divisors on }\Gamma_d(Q^A)\}\bigr)$). Manifestly nonnegative combinatorial model, new for all $m$-loop cases.

### 8.7 Quiver diagonalization (everything reduces to loop quivers)

J. Jankowski, P. Kucharski, H. Larraguível, D. Noshchenko, P. Sułkowski, *Quiver diagonalization and open BPS states*, arXiv:2212.04379, Comm. Math. Phys. 402 (2023) 1551–1584. Motivic DT invariants of any symmetric quiver $Q$ are encoded in a (generically infinite) **diagonal** quiver $Q^\infty$ whose only arrows are loops: $P_A(x) = P_{A^\infty}(x^\infty)$ under a monomial specialization $x^\infty_j\mapsto M_j(q,x)$ (survey Theorem 6.3). Consequence: $m$-loop quiver DT invariants are the universal building blocks of all symmetric-quiver DT theory; gives generalized integrality/LMOV proofs. Predecessor: same authors' "multi-cover skein" relation; related: P. Kucharski, H. Larraguível, D. Noshchenko, P. Sułkowski, *Unlinking symmetric quivers*, arXiv:2312.14905 (equivalence classes of symmetric quivers with equal $P_Q$, organized in permutohedra, governed by a universal quiver).

### 8.8 Attractor invariants and operadic wall-crossing

- S. Mozgovoy, *Operadic approach to wall-crossing*, arXiv:2101.07636, J. Algebra 596 (2022) 53–88. Plethysm/collections formalism conceptualizing wall-crossing transformation rules; streamlined proofs of known formulas and new formulas for attractor invariants.
- S. Mozgovoy, B. Pioline, *Attractor invariants, brane tilings and crystals*, arXiv:2012.14358, Ann. Inst. Fourier 75 (2025) 1331–1414. ## 10. What explicit formulas EXIST for $m$-loop DT invariants (bottom line)

1. **Numerical $\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n$ — fully closed form:** the Möbius/binomial formula (Thm 3.2, arXiv:1102.3978; survey Ex. 2.4(5)), plus polynomial-in-$m$ expressions for each fixed $n\le7$ printed there; equivalent counts by Lyndon classes, cyclic classes, Higgs sequences, break-divisor orbits; PSS closed form for arbitrary symmetric quivers (arXiv:1802.04573 Prop. 3.3).
2. **Refined $\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n(q)$ — closed *combinatorial* formula:** $\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n(q) = \frac{q^{n-1}}{[n]}\sum_{C\in U^{\mathrm{prim},+}_n/C_n}q^{\mathrm{wt}(C)}$ (Thm 6.8, arXiv:1102.3978; watch the misprint documented in §4.6 — as printed the prefactor is $q^{1-n}$, propagated into 1608.06600 (B.4)). Equivalent word/Lyndon models: arXiv:1608.06600 §4.1. **No product/hypergeometric closed form for all $n$ is known**, and there is provably no natural weight function on the counted classes realizing $\mathrm{DT}^{(m)}_n(q)$ directly as $\sum_Cq^{s(C)}$ (Remark after Thm 6.8).
3. **Structural identifications (all $d$, all symmetric $Q$):** $\Omega_d(q)$ = IC-Poincaré polynomial of $M^{ss}_d$ (arXiv:1411.4062; explicit $m$-loop version Thm 4.7) = Chow–Betti numbers of $M^{\mathrm{simp}}_d$ (arXiv:1512.03748 Thm 9.2) = dimensions of primitive parts of the free supercommutative CoHA (arXiv:1103.2736) = graded dimensions from zonotopal/break-divisor spaces (arXiv:2207.11861) = Poincaré series of a Koszul-dual Lie subalgebra (arXiv:2111.07588) = vertex-algebra data (arXiv:2108.10338).
4. **Reduction principle:** every symmetric quiver's refined DT theory reduces to (infinitely many) $m$-loop quivers via diagonalization (arXiv:2212.04379), which is why the $m$-loop case is the universal benchmark.
5. **Framed side:** $\chi$, Poincaré polynomials, and the full refined framed/unframed wall-crossing are closed-form (Fuss–Catalan; $q$-difference equations; $F = H(q,t)/H(q,q^{-1}t)$; Mozgovoy Thm 5.2; KRSS (3.6)–(3.7)); asymptotics: growth rate $\bigl(m^m/(m-1)^{m-1}\bigr)^n$ and Airy-law Betti distribution (math/0306185 Thm 1.5); algebraicity/quiver A-polynomials (KS Thm 3 limit; 1608.06600 (4.10)–(4.12); 1802.04573; 2004.10837).
